Slopume: Restoring Signal to the Job Search

I’m job hunting at the moment, and one small thing that always charged me up in the past was LinkedIn’s little red notification: “Someone viewed your profile.”

These days, LinkedIn seems to be serving a predominantly post-human audience, and the only notifications I get are people in my network playing games. Thanks for that, LinkedIn.

The Question
Are my applications even being looked at in a tough market where recruiters are buried with volume?

Answer: who knows? Let’s try to find out.
3 Tracked Links
24+ Alert Variants
58m Audio Detour

Tuesday night I added custom URL tokens to Slopume, my job search tool. Now every resume I generate includes application-specific links to my email, LinkedIn, and portfolio.

When someone clicks one, that event travels through a lightweight redirect service built with Vercel and Supabase before arriving on my phone as a push notification, complete with one of two dozen variations of the Love Island “I’ve got a text!” sound, set to round-robin playback. Gotta keep things fresh.

Now, I know what my technical audience is thinking: I agree. My suspicion is that many ATS platforms strip URL parameters when resumes are uploaded, so this won’t catch every interaction. But it will catch some, and that gives me signal.

As a happy side effect, it also partially de-ambiguizes LinkedIn’s “private profile” viewers. I don’t really care who viewed my profile—I care which company they work for, and this makes that a little more real.
